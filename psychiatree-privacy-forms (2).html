HIPAA Notice of Privacy Practices

THIS NOTICE DESCRIBES HOW MEDICAL INFORMATION ABOUT YOU MAY BE USED AND DISCLOSED AND HOW YOU CAN GET ACCESS TO THIS INFORMATION. PLEASE REVIEW IT CAREFULLY.

We are required by law to maintain the privacy of your protected health information (PHI), provide you with this Notice of Privacy Practices, and follow the terms of the notice currently in effect.

Who We Are

PsychiaTree is a psychiatric mental health practice operated by a board-certified Psychiatric Mental Health Nurse Practitioner and Family Nurse Practitioner licensed in the State of New Jersey. This notice applies to all protected health information created, maintained, or received in connection with your care at PsychiaTree.

How We May Use and Disclose Your Health Information

We may use and disclose your protected health information (PHI) without your authorization for the following purposes:

  • Treatment: To provide, coordinate, and manage your psychiatric care, including sharing information with other healthcare providers involved in your treatment (e.g., your primary care physician, specialists, pharmacists) with your knowledge.
  • Payment: To bill and collect payment for your care, including submitting claims to your insurance company or health plan (e.g., through Headway or other billing platforms).
  • Healthcare Operations: For internal activities such as quality assessment, practice management, training, and compliance audits.
  • Required by Law: When required by federal, state, or local law, including mandatory reporting obligations.
  • Public Health Activities: To report certain communicable diseases, adverse drug reactions, or abuse/neglect as required by law.
  • Serious Threats to Health or Safety: If we believe disclosure is necessary to prevent a serious and imminent threat to your health or safety, or the health and safety of another person.
  • Workers' Compensation: As authorized by and necessary to comply with workers' compensation laws.
Special Protections for Mental Health Information (New Jersey Law)

New Jersey law provides additional protections for mental health records beyond federal HIPAA requirements. Under the New Jersey Mental Health Records Law (N.J.S.A. 30:4-24.3) and the New Jersey Confidentiality of Mental Health Records Act:

  • Psychiatric treatment records are afforded heightened confidentiality protections.
  • Disclosure of mental health records generally requires your specific written authorization beyond standard HIPAA authorization — even for disclosures that HIPAA would otherwise permit without consent.
  • Psychotherapy notes (process notes) are kept separate from your general medical record and are protected by an additional layer of authorization requirements.
  • Your mental health information may not be disclosed to employers, law enforcement, or family members without your written consent, except as required by law or to prevent imminent harm.
  • Under N.J.A.C. 13:35-6.5 (governing APRNs), your records must be maintained for a minimum of 7 years from the date of the last patient encounter, or until the patient's 23rd birthday if the patient is a minor.
Uses and Disclosures Requiring Your Written Authorization

We will obtain your written authorization before using or disclosing your PHI for purposes beyond treatment, payment, and operations, including:

  • Most disclosures of psychotherapy notes
  • Marketing communications
  • Sale of your health information
  • Disclosure to family members, friends, or others not directly involved in your care
  • Disclosure to your employer or school
  • Disclosure to attorneys or for legal proceedings (except as required by court order)

You have the right to revoke any authorization you provide, in writing, at any time — except to the extent we have already relied on it.

Limits on Disclosure: Minors

New Jersey law (N.J.S.A. 9:17A-4) permits minors aged 16 and older to consent to outpatient mental health treatment without parental consent. In such cases, we will generally not disclose the minor's treatment information to parents or guardians without the minor's consent, except where required by law or when the minor's safety is at risk.

Mandated Reporting Obligations

As licensed healthcare providers in New Jersey, we are mandatory reporters under the following circumstances, which may require disclosure of your information without your consent:

  • Child Abuse and Neglect: N.J.S.A. 9:6-8.10 — any reasonable suspicion of child abuse or neglect must be reported to the New Jersey Division of Child Protection and Permanency (DCPP).
  • Elder and Vulnerable Adult Abuse: N.J.S.A. 52:27D-409 — suspected abuse, neglect, or exploitation of elderly or vulnerable adults must be reported to Adult Protective Services.
  • Duty to Warn (Tarasoff Obligation): Under New Jersey's duty-to-protect statute (N.J.S.A. 2A:62A-16), if a patient communicates a serious threat of physical violence against an identifiable person or persons, we may be required to take reasonable steps to protect the intended victim, which may include warning the victim and/or notifying law enforcement.
  • Imminent Danger to Self: If a patient poses an imminent risk of serious harm to themselves, we may initiate an involuntary commitment screening under the New Jersey Mental Health Code (N.J.S.A. 30:4-27.1 et seq.).
Your Rights Regarding Your Health Information
  • Right to Access: You have the right to inspect and obtain a copy of your PHI. Requests must be made in writing. We may charge a reasonable fee for copies. Under N.J.S.A. 26:2H-12.8, we must respond within 30 days.
  • Right to Amend: You may request amendments to your PHI if you believe it is inaccurate or incomplete.
  • Right to an Accounting of Disclosures: You may request a list of disclosures of your PHI for the past 6 years (or shorter period if requested).
  • Right to Request Restrictions: You may request that we restrict use or disclosure of your PHI, although we are not always required to agree.
  • Right to Confidential Communications: You may request that we contact you only in specific ways or at specific locations.
  • Right to a Paper Copy of This Notice: You may request a paper copy at any time.
  • Right to File a Complaint: You may file a complaint with us or with the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services Office for Civil Rights if you believe your privacy rights have been violated. We will not retaliate against you for filing a complaint.

New Jersey Law

Your New Jersey Patient Rights

As a patient receiving care in New Jersey, you have the following rights under the New Jersey Patient Bill of Rights (N.J.S.A. 26:2H-12.8) and the New Jersey Mental Health Bill of Rights (N.J.A.C. 10:37-6.1 et seq.):

  • The right to receive considerate, respectful, and dignified care regardless of age, race, ethnicity, religion, culture, gender, gender identity or expression, sexual orientation, disability, national origin, or source of payment.
  • The right to receive complete and current information concerning your diagnosis, treatment, and prognosis in terms you can reasonably understand.
  • The right to participate in decisions about your care, including the right to refuse treatment.
  • The right to be informed of all available treatment options and alternatives.
  • The right to give informed consent before treatment begins, including medication.
  • The right to have your medical records remain confidential and to control who may access them.
  • The right to access your own medical records within a reasonable time and at a reasonable cost.
  • The right to request a second opinion.
  • The right to receive an itemized bill and explanation of charges.
  • The right to file a complaint without fear of retaliation and to have it investigated.
  • The right to be treated in the least restrictive appropriate setting.

Informed Consent for Telehealth Services

This form provides your consent to receive psychiatric services via telehealth (video or telephone). Please review the information below about how telehealth works, its benefits and limitations, and your rights.

CONSENT FOR TELEHEALTH SERVICES — PsychiaTree

Definition: Telehealth involves the use of electronic communications — including secure video conferencing and telephone — to enable a healthcare provider to deliver clinical services to patients at a distance. PsychiaTree uses HIPAA-compliant platforms for all telehealth visits.

Benefits: Telehealth allows me to receive psychiatric care from a location of my choosing, reduces travel time, and may increase access to care. It is particularly useful for medication management, follow-up visits, and supportive therapy.

Risks and Limitations: Telehealth may not be appropriate for all clinical situations. In a telehealth session, the provider cannot perform a physical examination. Technical failures (poor connection, equipment malfunction) may interrupt or limit a session. In the event of a technical failure, my provider will attempt to contact me by phone to complete the session or reschedule.

Privacy: I understand that I am responsible for ensuring that I am in a private location during my telehealth session. My provider will take reasonable steps to ensure that sessions are conducted in a private setting as well. Sessions will not be recorded without my express written consent.

State Licensure: My provider is licensed in New Jersey. I understand that I must be physically located in a state where my provider is licensed at the time of each telehealth session. If I relocate, I will notify my provider so that my care can be appropriately managed.

Emergency Protocol: Telehealth is not appropriate for psychiatric emergencies. If I am in crisis, I will call 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline), 911, or go to the nearest emergency room. I will provide my provider with a local emergency contact and my current physical address at the time of each session.

Consent: I voluntarily consent to participate in telehealth services at PsychiaTree. I understand I may withdraw this consent at any time in writing and request in-person care or referral as appropriate.

Communication Consent & Preferences

At PsychiaTree, protecting your privacy is a priority in every aspect of your care — including how we communicate with you. Please read the information below carefully and indicate your preferences.

📱 Secure Messaging via Spruce Health

PsychiaTree uses Spruce Health, a HIPAA-compliant secure messaging platform, for all clinical communications. Spruce is free to download on iOS and Android. You will be invited to create an account before your first appointment.

All communications involving your health information — including medication questions, clinical concerns, lab results, and treatment discussions — will take place exclusively through Spruce.

UNDERSTANDING YOUR COMMUNICATION OPTIONS

Secure Messaging (Spruce Health) — Recommended for all clinical communication
Spruce is a HIPAA-compliant platform that encrypts all messages end-to-end, meaning only you and your provider can read them. It is specifically designed for healthcare communication and protects your protected health information (PHI) in compliance with federal law. All medication discussions, clinical questions, and sensitive health information will be sent through Spruce only.

Standard SMS (Text Message) — Administrative use only
Standard SMS text messages are not encrypted and are not HIPAA-compliant. While convenient for brief administrative reminders, SMS carries real privacy risks: messages can be intercepted, stored by mobile carriers, or accessed by others who have access to your device. For this reason, PsychiaTree uses standard SMS only for non-clinical, administrative purposes — such as appointment reminders, a prompt to log into Spruce, or a note that a secure message has been sent.

Examples of SMS use (administrative only):

· "Your appointment is tomorrow at 3:00 PM."
· "Please log into Spruce — I've sent you a secure message."
· "Your next appointment is available to schedule."

Examples of Spruce use (all clinical content):

· Medication questions, side effects, or refill requests
· Clinical updates, lab results, or care plan discussions
· Any message that includes your diagnosis, symptoms, or treatment details

Risk Acknowledgment: By consenting to receive standard SMS messages for administrative purposes, you acknowledge that standard text messages are not fully secure and that there is a risk that your information could be accessed by a third party. PsychiaTree will never send clinical or sensitive health information via SMS.

Email: Standard email is also not HIPAA-compliant and will not be used for clinical communication. Administrative emails (such as intake links or appointment confirmations) may be sent from a secure platform.

You may withdraw consent for SMS at any time by notifying PsychiaTree through Spruce. Withdrawing SMS consent will not affect your access to care.

Practice Policy

PsychiaTree Communication Policy

Clear communication supports safe, effective, and respectful care. The following policy outlines how PsychiaTree communicates with patients and what you can expect when you reach out. This policy applies to all patients and is effective from the date of your first appointment.

Download Spruce Health — Free & Required

All clinical communication at PsychiaTree takes place through Spruce Health, a free HIPAA-compliant secure messaging app. Download it on iOS (App Store) or Android (Google Play) before your first appointment. You will receive an invitation to connect with your provider through the app.

1. Platform Use
  • Spruce Health (Secure Messaging): Used for all clinical communication, including medication questions and refill requests, clinical concerns and symptom updates, care plan discussions, and any message involving your protected health information (PHI).
  • Standard SMS: Used for administrative purposes only — appointment reminders, prompts to check Spruce, and brief scheduling notifications. Clinical information will never be sent via SMS.
  • Email: Not used for clinical communication. Administrative emails may be sent via secure platform only.
  • Phone Calls: May be used for urgent clinical matters or follow-up at the provider's discretion. Voicemails will not contain clinical details.
2. Response Times

PsychiaTree is a telehealth practice and Spruce is a non-urgent communication channel. Please allow the following response times for messages sent through Spruce:

  • General clinical questions and non-urgent messages: Response within 1–2 business days (Monday–Friday, excluding holidays).
  • Medication refill requests: Please submit refill requests at least 5–7 business days before your medication runs out. Refills are not guaranteed outside of scheduled appointments and are managed at the provider's clinical discretion.
  • Scheduling and administrative requests: Response within 1–2 business days.
  • After-hours messages: Messages received outside of business hours will be reviewed on the next business day. Spruce is not monitored 24/7.

⚠ Spruce is not a crisis service. If you are experiencing a psychiatric emergency, suicidal thoughts, or are in immediate danger, do not send a Spruce message and wait for a response. Please call 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline), text HOME to 741741 (Crisis Text Line), call 911, or go to your nearest emergency room immediately.

3. Crisis & Emergency Communication
  • Spruce messaging is for non-urgent communication only and is not monitored in real time.
  • PsychiaTree does not provide crisis counseling, emergency psychiatric intervention, or after-hours urgent care via any messaging platform.
  • In any situation involving imminent risk to your safety or the safety of others, contact emergency services immediately.
  • If you are unsure whether your situation is an emergency, please call 988 or 911 — it is always better to seek support than to wait.
4. Medication Refill Requests
  • Refill requests must be submitted through Spruce only — not via SMS, voicemail, or pharmacy fax without prior arrangement.
  • Requests should be made at least 5–7 business days before your current supply runs out.
  • Refills for controlled substances (e.g., stimulants for ADHD) require an up-to-date appointment and cannot be processed between visits without clinical review.
  • Refills are not automatic and are subject to clinical judgment. Regular follow-up appointments support timely and safe medication management.
5. Scheduling
  • Appointment scheduling and rescheduling can be managed through your patient portal or by messaging through Spruce.
  • Please provide at least 24 hours' notice for cancellations. Late cancellations and no-shows may result in a fee as outlined in your Financial Agreement.
  • Appointment reminders will be sent via SMS and/or Spruce based on your stated preferences.
6. Professional Boundaries in Messaging
  • Spruce messages should be used for clinical and administrative communication related to your care at PsychiaTree.
  • Excessive, distressing, or inappropriate messaging may be addressed as part of your care, and boundaries will be maintained respectfully and professionally.
  • Your provider may not always be the one responding to administrative messages — a care coordinator or support staff may assist with scheduling and non-clinical matters.
7. Acknowledgment

By completing your intake forms and beginning care at PsychiaTree, you acknowledge that you have read and understand this Communication Policy. A copy is available to you at any time through this page or upon request through Spruce.

This policy may be updated periodically. Patients will be notified of material changes through Spruce or at their next appointment.
